# Notice Respond SEO Architecture

## Page roles

### `/`
The vertical pillar page owns broad Notice Respond / official-notice response intent. It explains what Notice Respond is, what kinds of notices it addresses, the general response process, what information users should gather, what happens after mailing, trust principles, FAQs, and a small curated set of representative workflows.

The homepage must not render the complete workflow catalog.

### `/workflows`
The workflow directory is the topical hub and discovery index. It owns workflow/type discovery intent, groups workflows by meaningful notice categories, provides crawlable links to every public workflow page, and explains how to choose the correct workflow.

The directory should scale to dozens or hundreds of workflows without becoming a wall of identical cards.

### Individual workflow authority pages
Each public workflow page owns one specific notice type or problem-driven search intent. It should answer what the notice is, who receives it, what information matters, what to gather, what Notice Respond can help organize, what the user should review, and what happens when the response is mailed.

Public authority pages should link to the workflow hub and related workflows, then hand off to the actual interactive workflow route.

## Internal-linking model

Vertical pillar → workflow hub → workflow authority page → interactive workflow.

Workflow authority pages should link upward to the hub and across to adjacent workflows where users may reasonably encounter both.

## Content rules

- Do not keyword-stuff.
- Do not create thin pages that only replace the workflow name.
- Use the user's actual search intent as the organizing principle.
- Do not invent legal deadlines, agency requirements, outcomes, or professional credentials.
- Keep product claims grounded in the implemented system.
- Keep AI advisory-only and distinguish generated suggestions from user-provided or document-extracted facts.
