## URGENT: ACT IMMEDIATELY

If you've received an eviction notice, time is your enemy. Most states require a response within **5-10 days**. Missing this deadline can result in an automatic eviction judgment against you.

**This guide is your step-by-step action plan for the next 72 hours.**

## Types of Eviction Notices: Know Your Enemy

Not all eviction notices are the same. Understanding which type you received determines your response strategy.

### Pay or Quit Notice
- **Deadline**: Usually 3-5 days
- **Reason**: Non-payment of rent
- **Your option**: Pay full amount due (plus late fees) to stop eviction
- **Response strategy**: If disputing the amount, respond in writing with evidence of payment or documentation of agreement

### Cure or Quit Notice (Fix or Quit)
- **Deadline**: Usually 5-10 days
- **Reason**: Lease violation (noise, unauthorized occupants, pet violations, etc.)
- **Your option**: Fix the problem within the timeframe
- **Response strategy**: Fix immediately and provide written confirmation to landlord

### Unconditional Quit Notice
- **Deadline**: Usually 5-10 days (sometimes 30 days)
- **Reason**: Serious lease violation or repeated violations
- **Your option**: Must vacate—no option to fix or pay
- **Response strategy**: File legal response claiming illegality or improper notice; prepare to move

### No-Cause Termination (In At-Will Jurisdictions)
- **Deadline**: Usually 30-60 days
- **Reason**: No lease term remaining or landlord chooses not to renew
- **Your option**: May be required to vacate
- **Response strategy**: Verify notice complies with local law; check for retaliation

## IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (First 24 Hours)

### 1. Don't Panic, Start Documenting
- [ ] Take photos of the notice as received
- [ ] Note the exact date and time received
- [ ] Note who delivered it (landlord, attorney, process server)
- [ ] Take photos of your rental unit (condition, any violations you dispute)

### 2. Read Every Word of the Notice
- [ ] Identify the notice type
- [ ] Find the response deadline
- [ ] Identify who to respond to (landlord, attorney, court)
- [ ] Find the address for service
- [ ] Note any referenced lease violations

### 3. Understand Your Local Laws
Your state's tenant laws are **critical**. Many evictions are illegal because the landlord didn't follow proper procedures.

**Research:**
- Visit your state attorney general's website
- Contact local legal aid (usually free for low-income tenants)
- Call your city/county tenant rights organization
- Search "[your state] eviction procedures" online

### 4. Gather Your Evidence
Start collecting documents that support your position:

**Always collect:**
- [ ] Copy of signed lease
- [ ] Rent payment records (checks, receipts, bank statements)
- [ ] Communications with landlord (emails, texts, letters)
- [ ] Photos/videos of unit condition
- [ ] Photographs of lease violations (if disputing them)
- [ ] Witness statements (from other tenants if applicable)

**For specific situations:**
- **Non-payment defense**: Proof of payment, evidence of agreed payment plan
- **Retaliation defense**: Proof you complained about repairs, health/safety, or exercised legal rights
- **Improper procedure**: Documentation showing landlord violated local requirements
- **Habitability claims**: Photos of mold, pests, broken utilities, heating/cooling failures

## RESPONSE STRATEGY BY NOTICE TYPE

### If You Received Pay or Quit Notice

**Option 1: Pay the debt (Simplest)**
- If you have the money and no disputes, pay immediately
- Pay to the address in the notice via certified mail or through established payment method
- Get written receipt showing full payment and "satisfaction of debt"
- This stops the eviction process

**Option 2: Respond in writing (If disputing amount)**
- Write a formal response letter
- Explain why you don't owe the full amount or why you've already paid
- Provide documentation (cancelled checks, payment receipts, written agreements for reduced rent)
- Send certified mail with return receipt

### If You Received Cure or Quit Notice

**Immediate action:**
- Fix the violation immediately (if possible)
- Document the fix with photos/video
- Send written notice to landlord: "I have cured the violation described in your notice dated [date]. See attached documentation."
- Send certified mail with return receipt

**If violation can't be fixed:**
- Respond in writing explaining why (e.g., "The notice cites unauthorized occupants, but persons listed are authorized under the lease")
- Provide supporting documentation
- Send certified mail with return receipt

### If You Received Unconditional Quit Notice

**Determine if it's legal:**
- In many states, landlords must have serious cause (repeated violations, criminal activity, substantial damage)
- Even "no-cause" evictions often require proper notice and waiting periods
- **Consult an attorney immediately** - this is your most serious situation

**File a written response claiming:**
- Improper notice (didn't follow state procedures)
- Retaliation (you exercised legal rights)
- Habitability issues (you have the right to a habitable unit)
- No legal basis for termination

## WRITING YOUR RESPONSE LETTER

If you need to respond in writing, use this format:

**Header:**
```
[Your Name]
[Your Address]
[Date]

[Landlord Name]
[Property Address]

RE: Response to Notice of Termination/Eviction
Received: [Date], Notice Effective: [Date]
```

**Opening:**
"I received your notice dated [date] regarding [property address]. I am writing to formally respond and contest the eviction as follows:"

**Body:**
Address each point in the notice. For example:
- If disputing amount owed: "I dispute owing $X. I paid $Y on [date] via [method], with receipt [reference]."
- If disputing violation: "The violation cited did not occur. I have evidence: [brief description]"
- If claiming illegal procedure: "Your notice fails to comply with [state law reference], specifically [requirement not met]."

**Supporting Evidence:**
"Attached documentation supports my response:
1. [Document] - [what it shows]
2. [Document] - [what it shows]
..."

**Closing:**
"I request that you withdraw this notice. I am prepared to [pay rent/fix violation/provide evidence] and remain in the property. Please confirm receipt of this response by [date]."

**Signature and Documentation:**
- Sign and date
- Keep copies
- Send certified mail with return receipt
- **Never send the original** - keep it

## PROFESSIONAL HELP: When to Get a Lawyer

**Get a lawyer immediately if:**
- You can't afford to move
- You believe the eviction is illegal or retaliatory
- The amount owed is substantial
- You have defenses (habitability, retaliation, procedure violations)
- The landlord has not followed proper procedures
- You don't understand your local laws

**Where to find free or low-cost help:**
- **Legal Aid** - Search "[state] legal aid" for your local office
- **Tenant Rights Organizations** - Most cities have free advocacy
- **Bar Association** - Your state bar has a lawyer referral service
- **Law School Clinics** - Often provide free services

## DEFENSES AGAINST EVICTION

### Retaliation Defense
In most states, landlords **cannot** evict you in retaliation for:
- Complaining to housing inspectors
- Requesting repairs for habitability issues
- Exercising legal tenant rights
- Joining a tenant organization
- Complaining about illegal activity

**Evidence needed:**
- Documentation of your complaint (email, letter, photos)
- Timeline showing eviction notice came within 6-12 months of complaint
- Proof the landlord received your complaint

### Habitability Defense
Every tenant has the right to a habitable unit with:
- Working heat/cooling
- Functioning plumbing
- No mold or significant water damage
- No infestations
- Working electrical
- Safe structure

**Evidence needed:**
- Photos of conditions
- Repair requests you made
- Medical records if health affected
- Witness statements
- Professional inspection reports

### Procedural Defense
Landlords must follow strict procedures:
- Proper notice format
- Required waiting periods
- Proper service method
- Court filing procedures
- All notice requirements

**Evidence needed:**
- Copy of notice received
- Your local eviction procedure requirements
- Documentation of procedure violations

## IF EVICTION GOES TO COURT

If you don't respond or your response is rejected, you'll face court.

**Prepare for court:**
- [ ] Bring all documentation
- [ ] Bring the notice and all correspondence
- [ ] Prepare a brief summary of your defense
- [ ] Arrive early
- [ ] Dress professionally
- [ ] Be respectful to the judge
- [ ] Request a continuance if you need more time (may buy you 30 days)

**At the hearing:**
- Explain your situation clearly and calmly
- Present your evidence
- Answer questions directly
- Don't interrupt the judge or landlord
- Request appeals rights and timelines

## EMERGENCY RESOURCES

**If you're being illegally evicted (forced out without court order):**
- Call police immediately
- Document everything (photos, videos, witness info)
- Consult attorney about unlawful detainer claim
- File complaint with landlord-tenant board

**If you can't afford rent:**
- Contact local rent assistance programs
- Call 211 (in US) for local assistance resources
- Reach out to nonprofits assisting tenants
- Some employers offer emergency assistance

## FAQ

**Q: Can I be evicted for retaliation?**
A: No. In most states, retaliatory evictions are illegal. Document your complaint and the timeline.

**Q: What if I can't afford a lawyer?**
A: Contact legal aid or tenant rights organizations. Many offer free services for low-income tenants.

**Q: Do I have to leave immediately?**
A: Not without a court judgment. An eviction notice is not a removal order. Ignore illegal self-help eviction.

**Q: Can I stay if I pay?**
A: If it's a Pay or Quit notice and you pay the full amount, yes—the eviction stops.

## Conclusion

Eviction notices are scary, but they're not automatic removal. You have legal rights, defenses, and time to respond. **Act immediately**, gather evidence, and understand your options.

Many tenants successfully fight evictions by responding quickly and professionally. Don't assume you'll lose—many landlords count on tenants not responding.
