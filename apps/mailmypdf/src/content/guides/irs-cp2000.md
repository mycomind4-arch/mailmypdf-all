## What is a CP2000 Notice?

The IRS CP2000 is a **Correspondence Examination notice** that means:
- The IRS found a **discrepancy** between your filed return and IRS records
- Typically: income reported to IRS (W-2s, 1099s, etc.) doesn't match what you reported
- The IRS is offering you a chance to **respond** before assessing additional taxes
- This is your opportunity to challenge the discrepancy or agree

## Why You Received This Notice

Common reasons for CP2000:

### Unreported Income
- W-2 wages the employer reported but you didn't include
- 1099 income (interest, dividends, business) not on your return
- Cryptocurrency gains IRS tracked
- Retirement distributions

### Timing Mismatches
- Income reported in wrong year
- Deductions claimed in wrong year
- Estimated tax payments credited incorrectly

### Missing W-2s or 1099s
- Forms issued but not received
- Forms received after filing
- Multiple copies of same income

### Underreported Income
- Discrepancy between what you reported and what was reported to IRS

## URGENT: Know Your Deadline

**You typically have 30 days to respond.** This is critical—missing the deadline can result in:
- IRS assessment of additional taxes
- Automatic penalty (20% accuracy-related penalty)
- Interest accrual from original due date
- No further opportunity to dispute

**Action**: Mark your calendar NOW with deadline date.

## Step 1: Understand the Discrepancy (Days 1-3)

Read your CP2000 letter carefully:

**Identify:**
- Tax year in question
- Specific income item(s) at issue
- Amount IRS says you underreported
- Supporting documents referenced
- Deadline for response

**What to look for:**
- Is this income you actually received?
- Did you already report it under a different form/amount?
- Is there a legitimate explanation for the difference?

## Step 2: Gather Documentation (Days 3-10)

**Always collect:**
- Your filed tax return (copy you filed)
- 1098/1099 forms you received
- Statements from financial institutions
- Correspondence with the income source
- Evidence of payments (cancelled checks, receipts)

**For specific situations:**
- **Unreported W-2**: Employer's 1099, employment records, cancelled paychecks
- **1099 Income**: Bank statements, invoice records, business records
- **Deductions**: Receipts, invoices, cancelled checks

## Step 3: Analyze the Discrepancy

### Type A: You Didn't Report It
If the IRS is correct—you forgot to include income:
- Prepare to amend your return
- Calculate additional tax owed
- Gather documentation supporting the income
- Prepare response accepting the adjustment

### Type B: You Already Reported It
If you included this income somewhere on your return:
- Find where you reported it (line number)
- Provide copy of that section
- Explain the discrepancy (different form used, timing issue, etc.)
- Reference the specific line on your return

### Type C: You Received Different Amount
If the IRS amount differs from what you received:
- Get documentation of actual amount received
- Explain why it differs (returns, refunds, corrections, etc.)
- Provide evidence (bank statements, amended 1099s, etc.)

## Step 4: Write Your Response Letter

**Format:**

```
[Your Name]
[Your Address]
[Date]

Internal Revenue Service
[Address from CP2000]

RE: Response to CP2000 Notice
Tax Year: [Year]
Taxpayer ID: [SSN]
Notice Number: [CP2000 #]

Dear IRS:

I am writing to respond to the Correspondence Examination notice dated [date] regarding discrepancies on my [year] tax return.

[Choose your situation:]

SITUATION 1 - Already Reported:
I included the income in question on my return as follows:
- Income: [Description]
- Amount: [Amount]
- Reported on: Line [#] of Form [#]
- Copy attached: Yes

The discrepancy appears to result from [explanation], which is properly reflected in my filing.

SITUATION 2 - Different Amount:
I received [actual amount] for this income, not [IRS amount]. Documentation attached shows:
- Bank statement showing deposit: [attached]
- Amended 1099 issued: [attached]
- Actual amount reportable: [amount]

SITUATION 3 - Accept Adjustment:
Upon review, I accept the IRS's finding. I will:
- File amended return showing correct income
- Pay any additional tax due
- Accept applicable penalties and interest

Attached documentation supports my response:
1. [Document] - [what it shows]
2. [Document] - [what it shows]

I request acceptance of this response and closure of this examination.

Sincerely,
[Your Signature]
[Your Name]
```

## If CP2000 is Wrong: Defenses

### Defense 1: You Already Reported It
**Evidence needed:**
- Copy of tax return showing income reported
- Documentation of which line/form
- Explanation of apparent discrepancy

### Defense 2: You Didn't Receive It
**Evidence needed:**
- No 1099 or W-2 received from source
- Correspondence with source requesting documentation
- Proof you didn't receive form

### Defense 3: Amount is Wrong
**Evidence needed:**
- Bank statements showing actual amount
- Amended 1099s issued by source
- Documentation of corrections (refunds, returns, etc.)

### Defense 4: Timing Issue
**Evidence needed:**
- Documentation showing income was in different year
- Amended returns showing correction
- Explanation of timing mismatch

## After You Submit Your Response

**What happens:**
- IRS receives your response (typically 2-3 weeks)
- IRS reviews your documentation
- IRS either:
  - ✅ Agrees with you (examination closes)
  - ⚠️ Partially agrees (partial adjustment)
  - ❌ Disagrees (assessment issued)

**Timeline:**
- Typically 60-90 days for response
- You'll receive written determination
- Notice of deficiency issued if IRS disagrees
- You then have rights to appeal or pay

## If IRS Disagrees: Next Steps

### Option 1: Request Appeals Conference
If you disagree with IRS determination:
- Request appeals conference
- Have 30 days from notice
- Can present new evidence
- Independent IRS review

### Option 2: Pay and File Refund Claim
- Pay the assessed amount
- File Form 1040-X (amended return)
- File refund claim (Form 1120-X for business)
- If denied, sue in Tax Court

### Option 3: Tax Court
- File petition (typically within 90 days)
- Dispute before IRS assessment
- No need to pay first
- Formal legal proceeding

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Missing the deadline (automatic loss)
- ❌ Not providing documentation
- ❌ Not addressing the specific discrepancy
- ❌ Providing original documents (send copies only)
- ❌ Not keeping copies for yourself
- ❌ Sending response via regular mail (use certified)

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a tax professional?**
A: Not required, but helpful if your situation is complex or the amount is substantial.

**Q: What if I can't find documentation?**
A: Explain what happened and what you do have. Reasonable explanation with partial documentation may help.

**Q: Can I ignore the CP2000?**
A: No—IRS will assess additional tax, penalties, and interest without your response.

**Q: What if the amount is small?**
A: Even small amounts matter. Respond to avoid penalties and interest accrual.

**Q: Timeline for full resolution?**
A: Typically 120-180 days from your response to final determination.

## Conclusion

CP2000 notices are **not automatic audits**—they're IRS inquiries you can respond to and challenge. Most are resolved through correspondence without further IRS contact.

The key to success: **respond promptly with documentation** addressing the specific discrepancy.
