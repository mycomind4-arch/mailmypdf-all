## Why Your Credit Report Matters

Your credit report is a financial record used by lenders to decide whether to give you credit, what interest rate to charge, and what terms to offer. **Even one error can cost you thousands of dollars in higher interest rates.**

Studies show that **1 in 4 Americans** has an error on their credit report. The good news: you have powerful legal rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) to dispute and fix these errors.

## What You Have the Right to Dispute

Under the FCRA, you can dispute almost anything on your credit report, including:

- **Wrong balances** (account shows $5,000 owed when you paid it off)
- **Wrong payment history** (marked as late when you paid on time)
- **Duplicate accounts** (same debt listed multiple times)
- **Accounts you don't recognize** (possible identity theft)
- **Closed accounts still showing as open**
- **Incorrect personal information** (wrong employer, address)
- **Accounts with wrong dates** (showing payment date incorrectly)
- **Identity theft accounts** (accounts opened fraudulently)

## Step-by-Step Dispute Process

### Step 1: Get Your Credit Report (Free)

You're entitled to a free credit report from each of the three major bureaus every 12 months.

**Visit**: www.annualcreditreport.com (the ONLY official site—avoid scams)

**Get reports from:**
- Equifax
- Experian
- TransUnion

**Tip**: Pull one report every 4 months to monitor changes throughout the year.

### Step 2: Review Carefully and Identify Errors

Print out or download your reports. Read them thoroughly:

**Look for:**
- ❌ Accounts you don't recognize
- ❌ Wrong balances
- ❌ Wrong payment statuses (30/60/90 day late vs. on-time)
- ❌ Duplicate accounts
- ❌ Accounts that should be closed but show open
- ❌ Personal information errors
- ❌ Accounts over 7 years old (should be removed)

**Document each error:**
- Account name and number
- The error (what it says vs. what it should say)
- Why it's wrong

### Step 3: Gather Evidence

Before disputing, collect documentation proving the error:

**For balance disputes:**
- [ ] Current account statements
- [ ] Loan documents
- [ ] Payment confirmation receipts
- [ ] Paid-off documentation

**For payment status disputes:**
- [ ] Cancelled checks
- [ ] Bank statements showing payment
- [ ] Payment confirmation letters
- [ ] Emails confirming payments

**For accounts you don't recognize:**
- [ ] Take notes of account details
- [ ] Document you don't recognize it
- [ ] Check for identity theft (unusual accounts)

**For identity theft:**
- [ ] Police report
- [ ] FTC Identity Theft Report (identitytheft.gov)
- [ ] Proof you didn't open the account

### Step 4: Dispute with the Credit Bureaus

You can dispute by mail, phone, or online. **Mail is best** because it creates a paper trail.

**The Dispute Letter:**

```
[Your Name]
[Your Address]
[Your Phone]
[Date]

Equifax
Attn: Disputes Department
[Address from credit report]

RE: Dispute of Inaccurate Information
Account #: [Your account number, if shown on report]

Dear Sir/Madam:

I am writing to formally dispute inaccurate information on my credit report. After reviewing my report dated [date], I have identified the following errors:

ACCOUNT: [Account name/number]
ERROR: [What the report says]
CORRECT INFORMATION: [What it should say]
REASON: [Why it's wrong - explain clearly]
ATTACHED EVIDENCE: [List attached documents]

[Repeat for each error]

I have attached documentation supporting my dispute:
1. [Document 1]
2. [Document 2]
etc.

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), I request that you:
1. Investigate this dispute
2. Correct any inaccurate information
3. Remove any unverifiable information
4. Provide me with corrected reports

I expect your response within 30 days, as required by law.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Printed Name]

Enclosures: [List all attached documents]
```

**Important:**
- [ ] Send **certified mail with return receipt**
- [ ] Keep copies of everything
- [ ] Include copies of supporting evidence (not originals)
- [ ] Don't include originals—keep them
- [ ] Attach a copy of your credit report with errors highlighted

### Step 5: Dispute with the Original Creditor

You should also dispute directly with the company that reported the error.

**Find the company:**
- Look at your credit report for contact info
- Search online for "Company Name" + "customer service"
- Call the company to find dispute department

**Send them:**
- The same dispute letter (modified to address them)
- Your documentation
- Certified mail with return receipt

**Why this works:**
- They may immediately correct the error
- Creates additional pressure for correction
- Shows the credit bureau you've notified the source

### Step 6: Monitor Your Results

The credit bureau must investigate within **30 days**. You should receive:

**Within 30 days:**
- [ ] Written explanation of investigation results
- [ ] Corrected or removed information if error is found
- [ ] Statement that information is disputed (if investigation inconclusive)

**What can happen:**
- ✅ **Removed**: Unverifiable information must be removed
- ✅ **Corrected**: Wrong information is fixed
- ⚠️ **Disputed**: Marked as disputed (stays on report if creditor disputes)
- ❌ **Unverified**: Bureau says they verified it anyway (you can appeal)

### Step 7: Follow Up if Necessary

If the bureau doesn't remove the error or you disagree with their findings:

**You can:**
- [ ] Request they add your dispute statement to your report (statement of dispute)
- [ ] Send a follow-up dispute
- [ ] Escalate to manager at credit bureau
- [ ] File complaint with CFPB
- [ ] Consider attorney if error is major

## Your FCRA Rights

The Fair Credit Reporting Act gives you specific legal rights:

### Right to Access
You can get a free credit report once per 12 months from each bureau.

### Right to Dispute
You can dispute any information you believe is inaccurate or incomplete.

### Right to Correction
Inaccurate information must be corrected or removed.

### Right to Statement
You can add a statement explaining disputed information.

### Right to Know Reasons
You have the right to know why credit was denied and what score was used.

### Right to Removal of Old Debt
Negative information generally must be removed after 7 years (bankruptcy: 10 years).

### Right to Sue
You can sue a credit bureau or creditor for FCRA violations. Many lawyers take these on contingency.

## Dispute Tactics: Advanced Strategies

### Demand Verification
Creditors must "verify" (prove) disputed accounts. Send certified letter:

```
I dispute the accuracy of [account]. I demand you provide verification of this account by [30 days].

Under the FCRA, if you cannot verify this account, it must be removed from my credit report.

Send verification to:
[Your Name]
[Address]
Certified Mail with Return Receipt Required.
```

Many creditors can't verify old accounts and will withdraw them.

### Document Everything
- Certified mail receipts
- Phone calls (get names, dates, times)
- Emails (print and save)
- Online dispute confirmations (screenshot)

### Multiple Disputes
You can send multiple disputes over time. Each one restarts the 30-day investigation clock.

### File CFPB Complaint
If credit bureaus ignore you or violate FCRA:
- File complaint at **www.consumerfinance.gov**
- Free and powerful
- Creates official record of company violations
- Often triggers escalation

## Sample Dispute Situations

### Situation 1: Account Not Yours (Identity Theft)

**What to do:**
1. Get FTC Identity Theft Report (free at identitytheft.gov)
2. File police report
3. Send both documents with dispute letter
4. Bureau must investigate as fraud account
5. Has different timelines under fraud rules

### Situation 2: Wrong Balance

**What to do:**
1. Gather current account statements
2. Prepare explanation showing correct balance
3. Send dispute with account statement as evidence
4. Bureau contacts creditor for verification
5. Creditor must verify correct balance

### Situation 3: Account Closed but Showing Open

**What to do:**
1. Gather proof of closure
2. Send dispute letter with closure proof
3. Bureau verifies closure status with creditor
4. Credit bureau corrects status

## Impact on Your Credit Score

**Disputed accounts:**
- Marked as "disputed by consumer"
- Many lenders ignore them when making credit decisions
- Don't count against you the same way

**Removed accounts:**
- Improve your credit score immediately
- Especially if they were negative
- Can raise score 50-100+ points

**Timeline:**
- Positive impact usually within 30 days
- Full impact after removal posts
- May take 1-2 billing cycles to show on new reports

## Important: Don't Fall for Scams

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Services that promise to "remove" delinquencies (illegal)
- ❌ Companies charging upfront fees
- ❌ "Credit repair clinics" (you can do it free)
- ❌ Services offering credit rebuilding (you can do it yourself)
- ❌ Promises to fix bad credit overnight

**Legitimate help:**
- ✅ Non-profit credit counseling (NFCC.org)
- ✅ Your bank's credit services
- ✅ Free tools and templates
- ✅ Legal aid organizations

## FAQ

**Q: Will disputing hurt my credit?**
A: No. Disputing actually helps by correcting errors.

**Q: How long does it take?**
A: 30 days minimum. Often 30-45 days for full resolution.

**Q: Can I dispute by phone?**
A: Yes, but mail creates better documentation.

**Q: What if the bureau says the creditor verified it?**
A: You can send another dispute with more evidence, or file CFPB complaint.

**Q: Can the error come back?**
A: Rarely if properly removed. Monitor your credit report annually.

**Q: When should I check my credit again?**
A: After disputes resolve (30 days), then annually (every 12 months).

## Conclusion

**You have powerful rights under the FCRA.** Don't accept errors on your credit report. Following this systematic process, you can dispute inaccurate information and improve your credit score significantly.

Most credit bureaus and creditors count on consumers not disputing errors. When you do, they often must remove unverifiable information.
