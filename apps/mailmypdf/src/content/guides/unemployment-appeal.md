## Why Your Unemployment Claim Was Denied

Common unemployment denial reasons:

### Disqualifying Separation Reasons
- **Voluntary quit** (left without good cause)
- **Willful misconduct** (intentional rule violation)
- **Excessive absences** (pattern of missing work)
- **Poor performance** (failing to meet job standards)

### Eligibility Issues
- **Work during base period** doesn't qualify
- **Independent contractor** status (not employee)
- **Government employee** (special rules)
- **Student status** (some restrictions)

### Administrative Issues
- **Incomplete application**
- **Failed to report earnings** correctly
- **Fraud investigation** pending
- **Outstanding work requirement** not met

### Employer Dispute
- Employer claims ineligibility
- Employer contests separation reason
- Employer disputes employment status

## CRITICAL: Know Your Appeal Deadline

**Most states: 15-30 days to appeal.** Missing this deadline means automatic denial—no second chance.

**Action**: Call the unemployment office TODAY to confirm exact deadline.

## Step 1: Understand the Denial (Days 1-3)

Your denial letter includes:

**Key information:**
- Specific reason for denial
- Law/regulation cited
- How to appeal (address, method)
- Appeal deadline (CRITICAL)
- Contact information

**What to do:**
- Read every word
- Highlight the stated reason
- Note the deadline
- Research that specific reason

## Step 2: Gather Evidence (Days 3-14)

**Always collect:**
- Employment contract or offer letter
- Pay stubs (last 3-6 months)
- Termination letter from employer
- Communications about job separation
- Attendance records (if you have access)
- Performance evaluations
- Emails from employer
- Witness statements (coworkers)

**For specific situations:**
- **Voluntary quit**: Proof of good cause (health issues, harassment, unsafe conditions)
- **Misconduct**: Your side of story with documentation
- **Poor performance**: Emails showing support/training, performance reviews
- **Excessive absences**: Medical documentation if illness-related

## Step 3: Determine Disqualification Type

### Type A: Voluntary Quit
You left the job. To appeal successfully, you need:
- **Good cause** to quit (health, harassment, family emergency, etc.)
- Proof you reported the issue to employer
- Proof employer refused to fix it
- Proof you exhausted options before quitting

**Example**: "Employer refused to address health and safety violations despite multiple written complaints."

### Type B: Misconduct
Employer claims you violated rules intentionally. To defend:
- Challenge the violation occurred
- Show it wasn't intentional
- Show employer didn't follow discipline procedures
- Provide your explanation

**Example**: "I was not insubordinate—I was following the updated procedure I wasn't trained on."

### Type C: Performance Issues
Employer says you couldn't do the job. To defend:
- Show you were meeting standards
- Show lack of training/support
- Provide performance evaluations
- Show change in job duties without notification

## Step 4: Write Your Appeal Letter

**Format:**

```
[Your Name]
[Your Address]
[Phone]
[Date]

Department of Employment
[Address from denial letter]

RE: Appeal of Unemployment Benefits Denial
Claim Number: [Number]
Claimant Name: [Your Name]
Claimant ID: [ID Number]
Employer: [Company Name]
Appeal Date: [Date of this letter]

Dear Unemployment Examiner:

I am writing to appeal the denial of my unemployment benefits dated [date]. I respectfully disagree with the determination and am providing this response to address the specific reasons cited in the denial.

REASON FOR DENIAL CITED: [Reason stated in denial letter]

MY RESPONSE:
[Clearly address the reason. Choose your situation:]

FOR VOLUNTARY QUIT:
I did not voluntarily abandon my job. I left due to [reason], which constituted good cause. Specifically:
- [Specific circumstance #1]
- [Specific circumstance #2]
- [Action I took to resolve: reported to management, etc.]
- [Why employer's response was inadequate]

I was forced to choose between [unacceptable condition] and my job. Attached documentation supports this.

FOR MISCONDUCT CHARGE:
I dispute the allegation of [misconduct]. The facts are:
- [What actually happened]
- [My side of the situation]
- [Employer's role in the situation]
- [Why it wasn't intentional violation]

I have been a responsible employee and this incident does not represent my job performance.

FOR PERFORMANCE ISSUE:
I was meeting job performance standards. Evidence shows:
- [Performance evaluation showing satisfactory performance]
- [Specific accomplishments/metrics met]
- [Lack of prior discipline or warnings]
- [Circumstances beyond my control: training, equipment, supervision]

ATTACHED EVIDENCE:
1. [Document] - [What it shows]
2. [Document] - [What it shows]
3. [Witness statement] - [Why relevant]

I am eligible for unemployment benefits under [state] law because [your situation]. I request that the denial be reversed and benefits be paid from [date] forward.

Respectfully,
[Your Signature]
[Your Typed Name]

Enclosures: [List of attached documents]
```

## Step 5: Submit Your Appeal

**Critical steps:**
- [ ] Type or print neatly
- [ ] Gather copies of all supporting evidence
- [ ] Include copies of relevant portions of employment records
- [ ] Send via certified mail with return receipt
- [ ] Send to exact address on denial letter
- [ ] Keep copies of everything

**Timeline:**
- Submit within deadline (typically 15-30 days)
- State will set hearing date (typically 30-60 days)
- You'll receive notice of hearing
- Hearing will be conducted (phone or in-person)

## Step 6: Prepare for Your Hearing

**Before the hearing:**
- Review your appeal letter
- Organize all evidence
- Prepare key points (bullet list)
- Identify witnesses who can testify
- Contact potential witnesses
- Practice your explanation (1-2 minutes)

**At the hearing:**
- Arrive early (or call 10 minutes before phone hearing)
- Bring all documentation
- Listen to employer's testimony
- Present your side calmly
- Provide evidence
- Answer questions directly
- Be respectful to examiner

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Missing appeal deadline
- ❌ Not providing documentation
- ❌ Emotional rather than factual presentation
- ❌ Not addressing the specific reason
- ❌ Bringing originals (send copies)
- ❌ Not showing up to hearing
- ❌ Contradicting yourself

## If Appeal is Denied: Next Options

### Request Further Appeal
Some states allow additional appeals:
- Board of Review appeal
- Administrative Law Judge review
- Request process from denial

### File Complaint
- Labor board complaint against employer
- Wage claim if wages owed
- Retaliation complaint if applicable

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a lawyer?**
A: No, but you can have representation if allowed in your state.

**Q: Can I appeal if I missed the deadline?**
A: Possibly, with good cause for late filing (illness, military service, etc.). Contact unemployment office immediately.

**Q: What if employer doesn't show up?**
A: Examiner may award benefits in your favor, or reschedule hearing.

**Q: How long until decision?**
A: Typically 2-4 weeks after hearing.

**Q: Can I appeal a second time if I lose?**
A: Usually one appeal, but may have higher-level review options.

## Conclusion

Unemployment benefits are **yours if you qualify**. An initial denial doesn't mean you don't qualify—it means the state needs more information.

Most appeals succeed when you:
1. Submit evidence supporting your version
2. Address the specific denial reason
3. Present yourself professionally
4. Meet all deadlines

Don't give up on benefits you're entitled to.
